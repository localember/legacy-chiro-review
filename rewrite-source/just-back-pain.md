Disc injuries are fairly common and can be very painful. It helps to understand how the spine and its discs work and how they affect overall spinal health, especially when discs are herniated or thinning from a degenerative disease like arthritis.

## The spinal disc
Between each pair of vertebrae sits a cushion called an intervertebral disc. It is made of cartilage-like material with a soft, gel-like center inside a tougher ligament wall that surrounds the whole disc. Attached to the vertebrae, discs absorb shock and keep the bones from rubbing. Besides cushioning impact, they help keep the spine both flexible and very strong.

## Bulging, herniated and ruptured discs
In a bulging or protruding disc, the gel center is pressing outward against the tougher outer wall, but the wall has not torn. Nearly 60% of adults actually have at least one bulging disc without any symptoms or pain. Once the gel starts tearing through the outer wall, the disc has moved from protruding to herniated. Whether it bulges or herniates, a damaged disc may irritate a nerve and bring inflammation, numbness and, in many cases, intense pain.

A ruptured disc, or disc extrusion, is a serious condition in which the gel tears all the way through the outer wall. Once that material leaks out, people often find themselves in serious pain and barely able to move.
