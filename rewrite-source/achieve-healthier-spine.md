H1: How to build a healthier spine, from your Cary chiropractor
How often do you think about your spine in a day? For most of us in Cary, the honest answer is never, as long as we can get through the day. People with back pain are the exception. When your back hurts, or you are living with an old injury, it is hard to think about anything else, between the pain and everything it sets off. Back pain costs you, both financially and emotionally.

Statistics show that roughly 80 to 90% of people will deal with spine problems or back pain at some point in their lives. Setting accident injuries aside, the people most at risk are smokers, people who are overweight, and people who lift heavy loads. With odds like that, a healthy lifestyle and a healthy spine are well worth working toward.

## Diet and exercise
- Learn your ideal weight and try to stay within 10 pounds of it. Extra weight stresses every part of your frame, the spine included.
- Build your core. Weak core muscles can pull the whole body out of alignment, beginning with the low back.
- Eat sensible portions of healthy food, and pair it with an exercise routine that fits your schedule. You will see the difference in your weight, your health and your confidence.
- Warm up with stretches before exercise and cool down with them afterward.
- Check with your physician before starting a new diet or exercise plan, since your health history may call for professional guidance.

## Quit smoking
Smokers heal far more slowly than nonsmokers. The more than 4000 chemicals in cigarettes sharply reduce blood flow throughout the body, which slows healing everywhere, including the spine.

## Sleep
- Support your body while you sleep. Pain is never normal, so if you hurt while trying to rest, find a position that relieves it.
- Sleeping on your back adds close to 50 pounds of pressure to the spine. A supportive pillow under your knees can cut that roughly in half.
- Side sleepers can ease spinal pressure with a pillow between the knees.

## Standing
- Locked knees load the lower back. Soften your stance with one foot slightly ahead of the other and a slight bend in the knees.
- Do not stay bent forward at the waist; it puts extra strain on the spine.

## Lifting
- Let your legs do the work, not just your back.
- Never twist while lifting.
- If you can push or pull something instead of lifting it, do. Pushing beats pulling, and use your legs to spare your back.
- Ask for help.
- When carrying something, hold it close and split the weight evenly between both hands.

## Sitting
- Do not slouch. Keep your shoulders back and your spine tall, without letting the shoulders round or the low back flatten.
- Keep your knees slightly above your hips.
- Hold your head up and look straight ahead.

## Bending and reaching
- To pick up something below waist height, bend your knees, not your waist.
- For anything above shoulder height, use a step stool. Overreaching can injure the neck, shoulders and mid-back.
- Get as close to the object as you reasonably can to limit strain.
