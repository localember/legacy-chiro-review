H1: How much schooling does a Cary chiropractor have?
Ever wondered how long your Cary chiropractor was in school, or how that training stacks up against other health professionals? Knowing a doctor's education and extra certifications often helps you choose the right provider. The title "doctor" suggests years of study, but every kind of health specialist follows a different training path before practicing. Our doctors wrote this article to explain what chiropractic training involves and how it can affect your care.

## What training is required?
As with many professions, exact requirements differ by state. Chiropractors are typically expected to finish undergraduate pre-medical coursework and earn a bachelor's degree before entering a postgraduate chiropractic college. Each of those colleges has gone through a detailed review to be recognized by the Department of Education as an accredited school.

Time spent at chiropractic college is substantial: at least 4,200 hours before graduation, split among classroom, lab and clinic, covering chiropractic and health care in general.

Classroom subjects include anatomy, physiology, diagnosis, pathology, biochemistry, neuromuscular and orthopedic evaluation, pediatric and geriatric care, microbiology, nutrition, immunology, radiology, philosophy and clinical research. Students must learn and be tested on all of it before moving on to clinical work. After the academic portion, a student usually spends at least 1,000 hours in a campus clinic supervised by a clinic doctor, learning hands on to diagnose and treat real patients.

## Exams and licensing
Chiropractic students are tested many times. Beyond their college's own evaluations, chiropractors practicing in the United States must pass four national board exams covering basic sciences, clinical sciences, physiological therapeutics, diagnostic imaging, case management and clinical competency. After graduating and passing the boards, each chiropractor must also be licensed by their state.

Learning does not stop there. Depending on the state, chiropractors must complete approved continuing education on a set schedule after licensure. Wisconsin, for example, requires at least 40 hours every two years.

## How does it compare?
All of this shows your chiropractor knows far more about your health than how to treat a back. A comparison with medical doctors, the providers most people know, helps. A typical chiropractic student completes about 2,419 hours of academic work before clinical training begins, while most medical students complete around 2,047. Each field has its strengths: chiropractic students spend more hours on anatomy, embryology, physiology, biochemistry, diagnosis, x-ray and orthopedics, while medical students spend more classroom time on pathology, psychology and obstetrics.
