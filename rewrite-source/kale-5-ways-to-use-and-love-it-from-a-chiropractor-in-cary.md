At Legacy Chiropractic we always encourage a healthy diet full of fresh fruit, vegetables, nuts, seeds and good quality meat. Kale comes up a lot because it is so high in vitamins. Many people tell us they tried it and did not like it, or have no idea what to do with it. The Ballam family eats kale every week and loves it, but using it well takes a little creativity.

There are two main kinds. Lacinato, also called dinosaur kale, is blue-green, fairly flat and a bit leathery looking. Curly kale has tight, ruffled leaves and a slightly more bitter taste that cooks away. You can usually swap one for the other, though one works better in a few of the uses below.

## 1. Green smoothies
This is how we use kale most. Dr. Ballam was blending kale into fruit smoothies years before the rest of the world caught on. We do not follow a recipe; we toss fruit and vegetables in the blender. A few tips make a big difference:
- Bananas, pineapple and mango hide the vegetable taste well and give you a tasty tropical smoothie.
- Lightly steaming the kale can tame the bitterness and help you absorb more nutrients.
- Carrots, cucumber and celery also blend in nicely alongside kale.
- For omega-3s, add raw flax seeds or a scoop of coconut oil. You will not taste it, but your body will thank you.
- Want more protein? Almond butter, yogurt or coconut milk all work.

## 2. Sauteed kale
We make this often. If you are prone to kidney stones, cooked kale is the better choice because it is very high in oxalates.

Chop a few garlic cloves and sautee them in olive or coconut oil (coconut oil handles high heat better, though not everyone likes it). Strip the stems and chop the leaves. With enough oil in the pan, add the kale, cover and cook just a few minutes until it wilts and turns deeper green. Sprinkle with salt and serve. A splash of soy sauce or some bacon is nice, but it is great plain. We eat it at breakfast under a fried egg, in stir fries, on a baked sweet potato with bacon, or as a side.

## 3. Kale chips
Easy and delicious. Curly kale works better than dinosaur kale here.

You need 1 bunch kale, 1 tsp olive oil and 1 tsp salt. Heat the oven to 350 degrees and line a baking sheet with parchment. Cut out the stems and tear the leaves into bite sized pieces. Rub the oil over the pieces so every leaf is coated, and season with the salt. Bake about 10 to 15 minutes, until the edges brown but do not burn.

## 4. Soups
One of the simplest options: stir kale into homemade soup. Our favorite is sausage, potato and kale.

## 5. Salads
This is where kale can go badly. Raw chopped kale tossed straight into a salad is tough, chewy and bitter. It needs a little massage first (who does not?). Strip out the stems and break the leaves into small pieces. Put them in a bowl and add 1/4 tsp sea salt, 1 tsp lemon juice and 1 Tbsp olive oil. Massage until the leaves feel soft and silky; they will shrink by about a third and darken a bit. Then add your favorite toppings and dressing.
