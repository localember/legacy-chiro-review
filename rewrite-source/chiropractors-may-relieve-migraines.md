## What counts as a migraine
People often call any severe headache a migraine, but true migraines meet specific criteria. They are less common than tension headaches and rate moderate to severe in pain. They recur, last anywhere from four hours to three days, and affect more women than men.

To be considered a migraine sufferer, a person must have had at least five episodes lasting within that time range, with at least two of these features: throbbing pain, pain on one side, nausea or vomiting, sensitivity to light or sound, and pain that gets worse with physical activity. About 20 percent of people with migraines see a visual aura beforehand. The warning tells them what is coming but does not make it any less intense.
