H1: Your right to a Good Faith Estimate
If you are paying for care yourself, either because you have no insurance or because you are choosing not to use it, federal rules say you should know the expected price ahead of time. Providers must give self-pay patients a written estimate, called a "Good Faith Estimate."
- The estimate should cover the full expected cost of non-emergency items and services, plus anything tied to them, for example lab work, medications, equipment or facility charges.
- You should have it in writing no later than 1 business day before your appointment or item. You are also free to request one from us, or from any provider, before you schedule.
- When a final bill is $400 or more above the estimate, you have the right to challenge it.
- Hold on to your estimate. A printed copy or a phone photo both work.

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services explains these protections at <a href="https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises">www.cms.gov/nosurprises</a>.
