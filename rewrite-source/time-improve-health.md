Every year, hundreds if not thousands of people hurt their low back gardening or doing other yard work, and many end up at their Cary chiropractor. It happens a lot in spring, when people are eager to get outside and work on their yards after a long winter stuck indoors.

Hurting your back is fairly easy, especially when moving heavy things around the yard. Good chiropractic care is one of the first things to seek after an injury like that, but regular chiropractic care may help prevent many of these injuries in the first place, along with some basic safety habits.

## Tips to protect your back
- <b>Stay fit.</b> In most cases, the fitter you are, the less likely you are to get hurt. Fitness is always smart, and especially before demanding physical work. Good muscle tone and flexibility go a long way toward keeping you free of painful back injuries.
- <b>Lift properly.</b> Lift with your knees, with your feet in front of you about shoulder width apart. Never lift with your back; that is one of the main reasons people get hurt.
- <b>Use tools that fit you.</b> If you have a smaller build, use smaller tools and get help with things that are too heavy.
- <b>Ice and heat.</b> If your back feels tight or you have had an especially hard day, icing the muscles properly usually relieves much of the tension. Another option is to switch between ice, which calms inflammation, and heat, which loosens the muscles. Never put ice directly on your skin, and be careful with heat.

Seeing your chiropractor regularly is a good idea even when nothing hurts, and it can help you avoid back injuries and others while working in the yard.
