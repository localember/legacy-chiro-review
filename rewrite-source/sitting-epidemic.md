H1: The sitting epidemic and low back pain in Cary
Dealing with low back pain in Cary? It is a frequent complaint among people who sit for long stretches. Most employees work at a desk with little chance to move during the day, and the chiropractors at our Cary clinic have long told people who sit a lot to get up and move.

The American Medical Association has recently recognized the problem too, urging employers to make other arrangements so employees do not develop low back pain from sitting.

## A nation of sitters
Long hours of sitting have also left many people obese and dealing with related health problems. So much of daily life happens in a chair: eating, watching TV, working, gaming, driving and browsing the internet.

Research at Duke University found that employees with a BMI over 40 filed twice as many workers compensation claims, had medical bills 7 percent higher than workers with a lower BMI, and were likely to miss work 13 times more than the average worker without obesity.

## Tools to fight back
The American Heart Association is promoting an app called Hotseat, created by Fran Melmed, to fight obesity by interrupting long periods of sitting. It syncs with your work schedule and can be set to give you 2 minute breaks throughout the day.

Treadmill desks are another option, built into the workstation so employees can keep working while walking, which helps with low back pain. Walking after meals and parking farther from the door at work also lower your chances of back pain.
