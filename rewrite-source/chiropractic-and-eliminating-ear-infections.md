## Subluxations start early
Subluxations are spinal misalignments that disrupt the nervous system. They form when the body cannot keep up with physical, chemical or emotional and mental stress. For newborns, physical stress is the big one.

In the womb, a baby grows seven times faster than after birth, so molding and constraining forces from the mother's tissues (shearing, stretching, compressing, twisting and bending) can shape the developing spine. A misaligned maternal pelvis adds more. Breech, face, brow and transverse positions can also alter the spine.

## The birth itself
Even a delivery that goes well can strain a baby's spine. Hands, forceps or a vacuum extractor may be used, and the neck is most at risk when it is pulled while bent back, turned, flexed or tilted to the side. After studying 1,000 infants with birth trauma, Dr. Gutmann, a medical and manual medicine physician, reported that these pulling forces often caused upper neck subluxations. He proposed that the nerve dysfunction that follows could lower a child's immunity and contribute to common illnesses such as ear infections.

## A gentle check
Care ideally begins with mom before conception, so her spine, pelvis and nervous system give the baby the best start. After birth, we review the birth history, test reflexes and range of motion, watch posture, feel the spine, and use a nervoscope to find temperature changes linked to subluxations. Any correction is made by hand with the lightest possible touch. A baby's spine more than doubles in size after birth, and the first six years of growth are critical, so regular checks through childhood make sense.
