Notice of privacy practices for Legacy Chiropractic in Cary

Legacy Chiropractic, 201 Davis Grove Circle #106, Cary NC 27519
John Ballam D.C., (919) 363-2277
Effective date: 8-8-13

THIS NOTICE EXPLAINS HOW YOUR MEDICAL INFORMATION MAY BE USED AND SHARED, AND HOW YOU CAN ACCESS IT. PLEASE READ IT CAREFULLY.

We know how much privacy matters, and we are committed to keeping your medical information confidential. We keep a record of the care we give you, and we may receive records from other providers. We use these records to care for you or help other providers care for you well, to get paid for your services as your health plan allows, and to meet our professional and legal duties in running this practice. The law requires us to protect the privacy of your protected health information, to give you this notice of our legal duties and privacy practices, and to notify you if a breach of unsecured protected health information affects you. This notice explains how we may use and share your medical information, what rights you have, and what obligations we have. Questions about this notice can go to John Ballam D.C. at the number above.

A. How we may use or share your health information

We keep information about your health in a paper chart and on a computer. Together these make up your medical record. The record itself belongs to this practice, but the information in it belongs to you. Federal privacy law permits us to use your health information, or pass it along, in these situations:

  1. Your care. We use your medical information to care for you, and we share it with our staff and others involved in your care. For example, we may share it with another physician or provider who offers a service we do not, with a pharmacist filling a prescription for you, or with a lab running a test. We may also share it with family members or other people who support you through an illness or injury, or after you pass away.
  2. Payment. We use and share your medical information to get paid for our services. Your insurer, for instance, usually needs certain details from us before it covers a claim. We may also share information with other providers to help them get paid for care they gave you.
  3. Health care operations. We may use and share your medical information to run this practice: for example, to review and improve the quality of our care or the skills and qualifications of our staff, to get your health plan to approve services or referrals, and as needed for medical reviews, legal services, audits, programs that catch fraud or abuse and keep us compliant, and planning and managing the business. We may share it with our "business associates," such as a billing service, that handle administrative work for us. Each business associate signs a written contract requiring it, and its subcontractors, to keep your protected health information confidential and secure. We may also share your information with other providers, health care clearinghouses or health plans that already work with you, if they request it for their quality assessment and improvement work, patient safety work, population health or cost reduction efforts, protocol development, case management or care coordination, review of the skills, qualifications and performance of health professionals, training programs, accreditation, certification or licensing, or fraud and abuse detection and compliance. We may also share your medical information with providers, clearinghouses and health plans that take part with us in "organized health care arrangements" (OHCAs) for the OHCAs' health care operations. OHCAs include hospitals, physician groups, health plans and other organizations that together provide health care. Our Privacy Officer can give you a list of the OHCAs we belong to.
  4. Appointment reminders. We may reach out to remind you of upcoming visits. When you are not available, the reminder may go to your voicemail or to the person who picks up.
  5. Sign-in sheet. We may ask you to sign in when you arrive, and we may call your name when we are ready to see you.
  6. Notifying and talking with family. We may share your health information to notify, or help notify, a relative, your personal representative, or whoever is responsible for your care, about where you are, your general condition, or your death (unless you told us otherwise). In a disaster, we may share information with a relief organization so it can coordinate these notices. We may also share information with someone involved in your care or who helps pay for it. If you are able and available, we will give you a chance to object before we share, although in a disaster we may share even over your objection if we believe it is needed to respond to the emergency. When you cannot give or refuse permission, for example because you are unconscious or away, our clinicians will decide carefully what to tell your loved ones and others.
  7. Marketing. As long as we are not paid to do so, we may contact you about products or services tied to your care, case management or care coordination, or to suggest other treatments, therapies, providers or care settings that may interest you. We may also describe this practice's products or services and tell you which health plans we participate in. We may encourage you to keep a healthy lifestyle, get recommended tests, join a disease management program, give you small gifts, tell you about government health programs, or suggest during a visit that you buy a product or service, which may earn us a payment. We may also be paid an amount that covers our cost of reminding you to take or refill a medication, or of otherwise communicating about a drug or biologic you are currently prescribed. Beyond those cases, marketing uses of your information, and any paid marketing messages, require your signed permission first. That authorization will say whether we are paid for any marketing you approve, and we will stop future marketing to the extent you take back your authorization.
  8. Sale of health information. Your health information is never sold unless you first give us signed permission. That authorization will state that we will be paid if you allow the sale, and we will stop future sales to the extent you take back your authorization.
  9. Required by law. We will use and share your health information when the law requires it, limited to what the law requires. Where we are legally required to report abuse, neglect or domestic violence, to respond to court or administrative proceedings, or to respond to law enforcement, we will also follow the requirements described below for those situations.
  10. Public health. We may, and sometimes must by law, share your health information with public health authorities to prevent or control disease, injury or disability; to report abuse or neglect of a child, elder or dependent adult; to report domestic violence; to report product problems and medication reactions to the Food and Drug Administration; and to report exposure to disease or infection. When we report suspected abuse of an elder or dependent adult, or domestic violence, we will tell you or your personal representative promptly, except when our professional judgment tells us that doing so could seriously endanger you, or would alert a representative we suspect of causing the harm.
  11. Health oversight. We may, and sometimes must by law, share your health information with health oversight agencies during audits, investigations, inspections, licensing and other proceedings, within the limits the law sets.
  12. Court and administrative proceedings. We may, and sometimes must by law, share your health information in an administrative or court proceeding to the extent a court or administrative order expressly allows. We may also share information when we receive a subpoena, a discovery request or similar legal process if reasonable efforts were made to tell you about the request and you did not object, or if a court or administrative order has resolved your objections.
  13. Law enforcement. We may, and sometimes must by law, share your health information with a law enforcement official, for example to help find or identify a suspect, a fugitive, a material witness or a missing person, or to obey a court order, a warrant, a grand jury subpoena or other law enforcement purpose.
  14. Coroners. We may, and often must by law, share your health information with coroners investigating a death.
  15. Organ and tissue donation. We may share your health information with organizations that obtain, store or transplant organs and tissues.
  16. Public safety. We may, and sometimes must by law, share your health information with the right people to prevent or reduce a serious, immediate danger to someone's health or safety, or to the public.
  17. Proof of immunization. If you agree on behalf of yourself or your dependent, we will give proof of immunization to a school that requires it before admitting a student.
  18. Specialized government functions. Information may go to military or national security authorities, or to a prison or officers who legally hold you in custody.
  19. Workers' compensation. Workers' compensation rules may require us to release information. For example, if workers' compensation covers your care, we will send periodic reports about your condition to your employer. We must also tell the employer or its workers' compensation carrier about injuries or illnesses caused by work.
  20. Change of ownership. If this practice is sold or merges with another organization, the new owner will take ownership of your record and health information. You will still have the right to ask that copies of your health information be sent to another physician or medical group.
  21. Breach notification. Should unsecured protected health information about you be exposed in a breach, you will hear from us as the law directs. If you gave us a current email address, we may use email to send breach information. In some cases a business associate may send the notice, and we may also use other appropriate methods.

B. When we may not use or share your health information

Except as this notice describes, and consistent with our legal duties, we will not use or share health information that identifies you without your written authorization. If you authorize a use or disclosure for another purpose, you may take that authorization back in writing at any time.

C. What you are entitled to

  • Asking for extra limits. You may ask us in writing to restrict certain uses and disclosures of your health information. Tell us which information you want limited and what limits you want. If you ask us not to share information with your commercial health plan about items or services you covered entirely yourself, we will honor that request unless we must share the information for treatment or legal reasons. We may accept or decline any other request, and we will let you know our decision.
  • Confidential communications. You may ask to receive your health information in a particular way or at a particular place, such as a certain email account or your work address. We will honor all reasonable written requests that say how or where you want to receive these communications.
  • Inspecting and copying. With limited exceptions, you may inspect and copy your health information. Send us a written request saying what information you want, whether you want to look at it or get a copy, and, for a copy, the form and format you prefer. We will provide copies in that form and format if we can readily produce them, or in another format you accept. If we cannot agree and the record is electronic, you may choose a readable electronic copy or a paper copy. On your written instruction, we will send a copy to someone else you choose. We will charge a reasonable fee covering labor, supplies and postage, plus the cost of preparing an explanation or summary if you ask for one and agree in advance. In a few narrow cases we can say no. If we refuse to release records of your child, or of an adult in your care who cannot act for themselves, because we believe access would likely cause the patient substantial harm, you may appeal. If we deny access to your psychotherapy notes, you may have them sent to another mental health professional.
  • Amending or adding to your record. You may ask in writing that we amend health information you believe is wrong or incomplete, and give your reasons. We do not have to make the change, and if we decline we will explain the denial and how you can disagree. We may decline when the information is not in our files, when someone else created it (unless its creator is no longer available to amend it), if you would not be allowed to inspect or copy it, or if it is already accurate and complete. If we decline, you may send a written statement of disagreement, and we may write a rebuttal. Everything related to an amendment request will be kept and shared along with any later disclosure of the disputed information.
  • An accounting of disclosures. You may receive a list of disclosures of your health information made by this practice. We do not have to list disclosures made to you or under your written authorization; disclosures described in items 1 (your care), 2 (payment), 3 (health care operations), 6 (notifying and talking with family) and 18 (specialized government functions) of Section A; disclosures for research or public health that leave out direct patient identifiers; disclosures incidental to a use or disclosure the law otherwise allows; or disclosures to a health oversight agency or law enforcement official that has told us an accounting would likely interfere with its work.
  • Getting this notice on paper. You have the right to be told our legal duties and privacy practices for your health information, including the right to a paper copy of this notice, even if you already asked to receive it by email.

To learn more about any of these rights, or to use them, reach out to John Ballam D.C. at the number at the top of this page.

D. Changes to this notice

We may change this notice at any time. Until we do, the law requires us to follow the notice currently in effect. Once we make a change, the revised notice will apply to all protected health information we hold, no matter when we created or received it. A copy of the current notice is posted in our reception area, is available at every appointment, and is posted on our website.

E. Complaints

Questions or complaints about this notice, or about the way we treat your health information, can go to John Ballam D.C. at the phone number at the top of this page. If you are not satisfied with how we handle your complaint, you may file a formal complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services at OCRMail@hhs.gov. The complaint form is available on the HHS website. Filing a complaint will never be held against you.
