Can dehydration cause back pain? A Cary chiropractor explains

When the temperature climbs, so does your chance of getting dehydrated. What many people do not realize is that running low on fluids puts your spine at risk too.

Why your spine needs water

Your spine has 24 movable bones, and between them sit discs that are made up largely of water. Whenever you get dehydrated, your body borrows some of that water from your spinal joints. Do that often enough, year after year, and the joints can wear down, making bone spurs and narrowing of the spinal canal (stenosis) more likely. That is the link between dehydration and back pain.

Ten ways to stay hydrated on hot days

  • Drink water. It sounds obvious, yet plenty of us fall short even on cool days. A simple guide: divide your body weight in pounds by two, and aim for that many ounces of water a day. If you are far from that number now, do not try to get there overnight. Add one more glass each day so your body can adjust.
  • Try coconut water. Sweat carries electrolytes out of your body, which is why it tastes salty. Coconut water replaces them, much like a sports drink, without the artificial colors or sweeteners.
  • Snack on fruit. A chilled slice of watermelon cools you down and hydrates you at the same time, with less sugar and no dyes compared to many frozen treats.
  • Step on the scale. Water makes up a big share of your body weight. A sudden drop in a short time can mean you are dehydrated. Weigh yourself before and after exercise so you know how much fluid to put back.
  • Check the bathroom. Going less often than usual on a hot day, or seeing dark yellow urine, can signal dehydration. You want a pale yellow, not apple juice.
  • Notice your muscles. Feeling weak, worn out or foggy? Your muscles may be running low, and a glass of water could be all it takes to clear your head.
  • Stay ahead of thirst. By the time you feel thirsty, you are already behind. Keep a water bottle with you and sip all day so thirst never sets in.
  • Do the pinch test. Gently pinch the skin on the back of your hand. Well-hydrated skin snaps right back. If it settles slowly, you may be dehydrated.
  • Pay attention to dry mouth. If your mouth feels parched, you do not need convincing. Go drink a glass or two.
  • Take dizziness seriously. Dehydration lowers the plasma volume in your blood, which affects blood pressure and can leave you light-headed. This can be a serious sign.

Drink to your spine's health

Next time you pass through the kitchen, top off your water bottle. Your discs, and the rest of you, will be grateful. Questions? Reach our Cary office at (919) 363-2277 or read about our back pain care.
