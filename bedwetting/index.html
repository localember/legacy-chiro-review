Chiropractic help for bedwetting in Cary

Bedwetting affects a lot of children, and it can be hard on them at any age. Kids often feel embarrassed, ashamed or guilty, and fear of being found out can keep them away from friends and family, which can hold back their social skills. For some Cary children it changes daily life: skipping sleepovers, or relying on diapers, plastic mattress covers or alarms.

When should bedwetting stop?

All of us start out wetting the bed. As kids grow, the bladder grows too, and they learn to control it. Most children should have bladder control by age 5, yet many children today still struggle at night. Only 15% of them will outgrow bedwetting without some kind of help. Chiropractic is not a "cure" for bedwetting, but ask the parents in our office how fast their child's bedwetting cleared up once care began. Many parents like that the approach is drug-free and gentle: the adjustment simply clears the way, and the child's body handles the healing.

How the spine and bladder connect

Two muscles control emptying of the bladder: the detrusor and the trigone. Their nerve supply branches off the spinal cord and exits between the bones of the lower back and the sacrum (the "tailbone"). Early in life, a child's sacrum is made of five pieces that slowly fuse into one as the child grows.

Learning to crawl, walk and ride a bike is exciting, and it also means lots of falls. Each fall is a small trauma that can shift the pelvis, the sacrum and the vertebrae of the spine. When that happens, pressure builds on the nerves leaving the spinal cord and weakens the signals they carry to the organs they serve. For some children, this nerve pressure is the real reason behind wet nights.

What to expect at Legacy Chiropractic

During your child's exam, we check the whole body for misalignments. Then we use light, hands-on adjustments made for small bodies to relieve any pressure we find on the nerves.

What studies have found

  • In a study of 165 children who wet the bed six nights a week, bedwetting fell to only three nights a week after just a few adjustments. By the end of the study, 20% of the children were considered "dry," with no more episodes.
  • A separate study compared 30 kids under chiropractic care with a control group of 20 kids who got no care at all. The care group saw a 25% drop in bedwetting episodes, while not one child in the control group changed.
  • Research continues to report good results for kids under chiropractic care. Bedwetting is one example; ear infections and ADD/ADHD are others, and the list keeps growing.

A controlled study of bedwetting and chiropractic

Forty-six children with nocturnal enuresis (bedwetting) took part in a 10-week study designed to evaluate chiropractic management of the condition. The treatment group received high-velocity, short-lever spinal adjustments consistent with Palmer Package Techniques. Children in the comparison group got a placebo instead: an Activator instrument on its nontension setting, pressed against the examiner's own hand at the contact point. The adjusting was done by a pair of fifth-year chiropractic students working under the supervision of two clinic faculty.

In the treatment group, 25% of the children cut their wet nights by half or more compared with their starting point. No child in the control group did.

The authors concluded that the results strongly suggest chiropractic treatment is effective for primary nocturnal enuresis, and that a larger, longer study with a 6-month follow-up is warranted.

Reed WR, Beavers S, Reddy SK, Kern G. Chiropractic management of primary nocturnal enuresis. J Manipulative Physiol Ther 1994; 17(9): 596-600.
