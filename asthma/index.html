Chiropractic and asthma in Cary NC

Living with asthma in Cary? You have plenty of company. Asthma diagnoses in the United States have climbed more than 60% since the late 1980s, and the CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) estimates that close to 20,000,000 Americans have it. Most care today relies on medication to manage symptoms and attacks, yet no drug offers a way to prevent asthma, reduce it, or improve an asthmatic's quality of life. The encouraging news for Cary families is that there is a drug-free option.

The nervous system and your lungs

Research points to a connection between breathing problems like asthma and the health of the spinal cord and nervous system. Start with one question: what runs your respiratory system? The nervous system does. A subluxation interferes with how that system works, and an adjustment removes the subluxation.

In one clinical trial, asthma patients who received chiropractic adjustments showed major improvement in bronchial hyperactivity, better control of asthma symptoms and less severe asthma.

How chiropractic helps

Your lungs and diaphragm sit inside the chest cavity. The diaphragm does most of the work of breathing, and it takes its orders from the phrenic nerve. The fibers that form the phrenic nerve leave the spinal cord in the middle of the neck, at C3, C4 and C5. When those nerves are pinched, messages from the brain to the diaphragm get disrupted, and with enough compression over time the nerves can eventually die. Chiropractic helps asthma by restoring normal nervous system function.

Subluxations can come from physical trauma such as birth, slips, falls, poor posture or accidents. They can also come from toxins tied to poor nutrition, smoking and drinking, and even from stress and negative thinking. Chiropractors are highly trained doctors whose job is to correct subluxations and help keep them from coming back.

The research

The Journal of Vertebral Subluxation Research published a study of 81 children with asthma who received chiropractic care for two months. Their asthma attacks dropped by 45%, and 31% chose on their own to cut back on their medication. These changes came from restoring nervous system function.
