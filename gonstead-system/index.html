The method

The five components of Gonstead chiropractic in Cary

We would rather spend a few extra minutes gathering evidence than guess. Each of these five checks answers a different question, and we adjust only where the answers agree.

Book a free consultCall (919) 363-2277

Why five checks instead of one

Any single test can mislead. A sore spot is not always the problem, and a picture on film does not tell you how a joint moves. By lining up five separate kinds of findings, your Cary chiropractor can point to one segment with confidence and leave the rest of the spine alone.

X-ray analysisPalpationInstrumentationVisualizationCase management

Component 1 of 5

X-ray analysis at our Cary office

We take X-rays standing up, from the front and from the side, on films long enough to show the whole spine in one view. Standing matters because it shows your spine while it is carrying your weight. Full-spine views also let us count the vertebrae accurately, judge disc spacing and curves, and spot issues that sit away from where it hurts. Not every patient needs films; the doctor decides case by case.

Component 2 of 5

Palpation at our Cary office

Here the doctor uses his hands, in two different ways. With you sitting or lying still, the doctor feels for swelling, tight or tender tissue and warmth (static palpation). Then he moves each segment through its range to see which joints are not gliding the way their neighbors do (motion palpation).

Component 3 of 5

Instrumentation at our Cary office

A dual-probe instrument, the kind Dr. Gonstead introduced with the Nervoscope, glides down both sides of your spine at once and compares skin temperature. An uneven reading can flag an irritated area. Repeating it on later visits gives us a simple, objective way to see whether things are settling down.

Component 4 of 5

Visualization at our Cary office

Our exam begins before you reach the table. We notice how you walk in, how you stand, and whether one ear, shoulder or hip sits higher than the other. A digital posture analysis puts numbers on what we see. These clues never stand alone, but they help confirm what the films and our hands are telling us.

Component 5 of 5

Case management at our Cary office

Your story matters: what you feel, when it started, what makes it better or worse, and what you want to get back to. We use that history to decide where to look first, never as a recipe. Then we pull all five components together, explain the findings in plain language, and write a care plan built for you.

Where the method came from

The system is named for Dr. Clarence Gonstead, a Wisconsin chiropractor with an engineer's eye. Read his story.
