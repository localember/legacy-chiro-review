The method

How the Gonstead system works

Dr. Clarence Gonstead was a Wisconsin chiropractor with a mechanic's mind. He wanted proof before he adjusted, and his system still asks for it today.

Book a free consultCall (919) 363-2277

A short history

Clarence Gonstead was born in 1898, grew up around engines and machinery, and graduated from Palmer College of Chiropractic in 1923. He went on to run a large practice in small-town Wisconsin and taught his methods to chiropractors from around the world.

Visualization

We watch how you walk, stand and sit from the moment you arrive. Uneven ears, shoulders or hips give early clues.

Instrumentation

A dual-probe instrument glides along the spine and compares temperature on each side. Differences can point to irritated areas and help us track progress over time.

Palpation

Static palpation checks contour, tone and tenderness with you still. Motion palpation checks how each joint moves as you bend.

Full-spine X-rays

Standing films taken from the front and the side show the whole spine in one view, under the load of your own body weight.

Your history

What you are feeling and when it started helps us decide which area matters most. It guides the exam but never replaces it.
