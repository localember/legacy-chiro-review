New patients

Your first chiropractic visit in Cary NC

Day one at Legacy is mostly about listening and looking. We gather the evidence first, because a Gonstead adjustment is only as good as the analysis behind it.

Before you arrive

When you book, our team will ask you to share your health history and what is bothering you. The more we know about past injuries, current symptoms and your goals, the better use we can make of your time in the room.

Your story comes first

You will sit down with your doctor and talk through what brought you in: when it started, what makes it better or worse, and what you would love to get back to doing. We want the full picture, not just the spot that hurts.

The five-part Gonstead analysis

Our doctors use the Gonstead method exclusively, which means several separate checks have to agree before anything gets adjusted. At your first visit that usually includes:

  1. Posture analysis. A digitized look at how you stand and where your body may be compensating.
  2. Temperature reading. A dual-probe instrument compares skin temperature on both sides of your spine to point out areas that may be irritated.
  3. Static palpation. Your doctor feels the tissue along your spine for swelling, tenderness or tightness while you hold still.
  4. Motion palpation. Your doctor checks how each segment moves as you bend, to find joints that are stuck or not moving well.
  5. Full-spine X-rays. Standing front and side views, taken in our on-site X-ray room when they are appropriate for you.

Why X-rays matter in Gonstead care

Standing films show how your spine carries weight in real life. They help your doctor see the alignment of each segment, the health of your discs and which direction an adjustment should go. That detail is what lets us adjust one level precisely instead of guessing.

Before you leave

Your doctor will explain what happens next in plain language, and we will set up your second visit, where we walk you through your results and your care plan. If you have questions at any point, ask. No question is too small.
